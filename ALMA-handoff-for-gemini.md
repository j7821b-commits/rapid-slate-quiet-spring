## What this is

**ALMA** = Assistance Ledger for Mutual Aid.

“Alma” is Spanish for soul. That is on purpose. “Mutual aid” here does **not** mean a new charity, a GoFundMe, or neighbors passing cash at the door. It means: find help the city and county **already funded**, hold it on **one ledger**, and get the paper to the household **through the people who already talk to them about the property**.

The demo is set on the Eastside of San Antonio, Bexar County. The building in the picture is a duplex at **118 Perez Ave**. Around it, 311 duplexes share the same ledger. Two households need a visit today. The rest stay quiet.

This is a demonstration. The families are fictional. It is not an official City of San Antonio, Bexar County, CPS Energy, or CAM office.

---

## The goal

People lose housing and lights not because no program exists, but because the help is scattered, the paper never reaches the door, and whoever does reach the door often looks like a collector, a stranger, or a raid.

The goal is simpler than it sounds:

1. Notice when a household is about to fall (eviction notice, utility shutoff).
2. Match them to a program that already exists.
3. Hold that match on one book so nobody has to “trust a guy with cash.”
4. Send a real person to sit with the tenant.
5. Send the signed paper through **property management / the office** — not a cousin, not a middleman, not a hand at the door.
6. If the process holds: the building still stands, and the people inside are still okay. Both. Not one at the expense of the other.

ALMA sits with the office and the tenant. Not against either. Not instead of either. The office, the household, and this tool are on the same side.

If a step does not hold, something in the process fell through, or it was never made easy enough to use. That is a design problem. It is not proof that someone did not care. Do not assign fault. Do not set the tenant against the office, or the office against the tool. Fix the process until people can actually do it.

## The task (what the prototype is showing)

There are two layers. They are not equal.

### The office / the ledger

This is where money and eligibility live.

- **In:** City and county programs first. Ministry partners next. A neighbor pool only if those fail. In the demo the neighbor pool is $1,250. It is last resort.
- **Ledger:** Matched once. Held so nobody at the door has to ask for cash or trust.
- **Out:** Rent paid to the office / landlord. Lights paid to the utility (in the demo, CPS). Never a middleman. Never a hand at the door.

The ledger is allowed to: look up the notice, match a program, fill the application, hold the money, submit the signed paper.

The ledger is **not** allowed to: knock on the door, pretend to be the tenant, or skip the office.

### The visit (Marisol Reyes)

Marisol is a **promotora**. Spanish and English. She works the Eastside lobby. She is the human being.

She is not a caseworker with a checkbook. She is not a cop. She is not a canvasser with a clipboard of “walk / match / money.”

At the door she does three things, in this order:

1. **Make time.** Go see them. Do not send a stranger.
2. **Confirm the tenant.** Talk to the person on this folder. Make sure it is them.
3. **Describe the help.** City programs, paid through the office that already talks to them about this property. Nobody here asks for cash.

Then they sit together. If the tenant agrees, they sign. Marisol witnesses. The paper goes back to the office.

She cannot:

- Hand them cash
- Change who qualifies
- Close the folder herself
- Send the help through a second party

Those buttons exist in the prototype on purpose. They are locked. If you press them, they say no, and they say why.

## The two open folders (the story in the demo)

**Unit A — The Garcia Family**  
118 Perez Ave, Unit A. Five people, two children. $2,850/month, below the 2026 family poverty line. Three-day notice to vacate. About $1,450 short (rent + late fees). Clock is running.

What ALMA does: match **City of San Antonio Rental Assistance** (up to $3,500 in the demo). Marisol goes to see them, confirms it is the Garcias, sits with them, they sign. Paper goes to the Perez Avenue office. Eviction clock pauses while the city pays **management**, not a hand at the door.

**Unit B — The Washingtons**  
Same duplex, other side. Five people, two children. $3,100/month, at the poverty line. CPS Energy disconnect. $480 past due.

What ALMA does: **Christian Assistance Ministry (CAM)** can help once per 12 months. Marisol sits with them, explains that the pledge goes to CPS, not through a neighbor’s pocket, and sends them to the CAM window with one bus pass. She does not sit the intake for them.

**120 Perez (Salinas)** — watch only. Optional check-in. No city folder.

**116 Perez (Okonkwo)** — already stable. Leave them alone unless they ask.

There is a small chart: what happens over four weeks if nobody comes (eviction fees, payday loans) versus if the visit happens on time. That is not a sales graph. It is the cost of delay.

## How it is supposed to feel

- Paper and plaster. Cream, ink, forest green. Like a housing office or a church desk. **Not** a black grid, not a “swarm,” not an AI cockpit, not military.
- Language a tenant would not flinch at. No “deploy.” No “analog payload.” No “fiscal authority.” No checklist that says Walk / Match / Money at the door.
- “Mutual aid” must never read as charity. The money is mostly **government programs that already exist.**
- The name ALMA stays. Do not rename it Swarm, Node, Task Force, or anything a bad-faith reader can turn into a raid.

If you give people a high-tech looking app, they will not trust it. This has to look like something they have already seen in an office.

---

## What “it works” means

Do not claim it works in the real world. This is a prototype of a process.

**If the process works, you should be able to see:**

- A household in friction gets matched to an existing program without inventing a new fund.
- The person at the door is someone who made time, confirmed the name, and explained the help in plain speech.
- Money never changes hands at the door.
- Paper goes through the property office — the same people who already collect rent.
- The building is still occupied. The household is still there. The office still has a tenant. That is the benefit. The complex does better **because** the residents are okay, not instead of them.

If a step does not hold, say what fell through. Say how to make that step easier. Do not say the office did not care. Do not say the tenant failed. Do not say the tool is the enemy. We are here to help. If it is not working, the design is not finished. That is useful. Do not paper over it.
